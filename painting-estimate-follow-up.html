Painting estimate follow-up: a respectful 14-day process

Painting estimates often involve choices about preparation, coatings, colors, scheduling, and occupied spaces. A useful follow-up adds clarity about those real project details instead of repeatedly asking whether the customer has decided.

Suggested schedule

  1. Day 0: Send the estimate and summarize the included surfaces, preparation, coats, products, and exclusions.
  2. Day 2: Confirm receipt and offer to clarify one scope question.
  3. Day 5: Add a relevant detail about preparation, product options, drying time, furniture movement, or scheduling.
  4. Day 9: Ask whether the project is still being considered and whether the scope has changed.
  5. Day 14: Close the loop and pause unless the customer engages.
Receipt-check example

Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Painting Business]. I’m checking that the estimate for [specific rooms or exterior surfaces] reached you. I’m happy to clarify the preparation or coating details if helpful.

Close-the-loop example

Hi [Name], I’ll pause my follow-ups on the [project] estimate so I don’t crowd your messages. If you want to revisit the scope or timing later, reply here anytime. Thanks for considering [Painting Business].

What to record

Track the customer, project area, estimate date, last contact, next action, next-action date, and outcome. Keep private customer information only in tools appropriate for your business and follow applicable messaging and email rules.

Keep every message accurate

  • Do not invent limited availability or price deadlines.
  • Use project-specific details only when they are true.
  • Honor requested timing, firm decisions, and opt-outs immediately.
  • Review every message before sending.
